## The headline claim does not survive its own control

The premise handed over was "v9 was stably above 1000, v11 lost ~100 points."
Both halves need correcting.

**v9 was never stable above 1000.** Its trajectory peaked at 1070.2 on game 42
and decayed monotonically to 994.9 by game 77. v8 peaked at 1108.3 on game 12
and settled at 1013.6. Both are the same shape: an early overshoot against a
weak opening draw, then decay as matchmaking finds stronger opponents. The
"1000+" figure was the top of the overshoot, not a plateau.

**The two identical v11 submissions scored 874.2 and 935.8** - 61.6 points
apart, same code, same deck, same hour. Fitting the rating-controlled model
below to v11a against v11b returns an implied **-75.5 Elo** for a comparison
where the true difference is exactly zero. That is the noise floor of a
45-game run, and it is most of the effect being explained.

## Raw win rate is inverted by opponent composition

v11 has the *highest* raw win rate of the four runs (0.61/0.60 against
0.57/0.56) and the *lowest* rating. Both facts are consequences of the draw:

| Opponent bucket | v8 | v9 | v11a | v11b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| <800 | 5/6 83% | 6/8 75% | 15/19 79% | 4/6 67% |
| 800-899 | 3/3 100% | 8/8 100% | 11/19 58% | 10/12 83% |
| 900-999 | 10/13 77% | 19/31 61% | 1/6 17% | 12/26 46% |
| 1000-1099 | 8/26 31% | 11/29 38% | **0/0** | 1/1 100% |
| 1100+ | 2/2 100% | 0/1 0% | **0/0** | 0/0 |

**v11a played zero games against 1000+ opponents. v11b played one.** v8 and v9
played 56% and 39% of their games there. Rating is won and lost in that band,
so v11 was never in a position to gain it.

This also voids the promotion gate v11's own `RESULTS.md` set, which required
"at least 60-80 games versus 1000+ opponents". The run returned 1. **The gate
was not failed; it was never evaluable.**

## Ruling out the mechanical failure modes

Each of these was measured, not assumed:

* **Latency / timeout.** All 89 episodes ended `('DONE','DONE')`. v11 consumed
  12.9s and 14.9s of the 600s overage budget per game (150 ms/decision against
  v9's 109 ms); the lowest `remainingOverageTime` ever observed was 577.0s.
  The search layer costs 38% more clock and is nowhere near the limit.
* **Illegality / crashes.** Zero illegal selections across 89 ladder games.
* **Deck drift.** All four runs report the single deck hash
  `9714ab5c3996f6cc`.
* **Under-rated opponents.** The obvious confound is that v11's 806-rated pool
  was really newcomers still climbing from 600. It was not: the mean absolute
  rating move of v11's opponents in our own games was 4.95 and 5.32 points
  (v8 4.65, v9 8.25), and **0%** of them moved more than 40. v11 faced settled
  agents whose ratings can be trusted.
* **Meta shift.** Archetype shares are stable: mirror 24.7% for v11 against
  37.7%/26.0%, Alakazam 22.5% against 22.1%/22.0%.
* **Game shape.** Prize margins are indistinguishable. In losses v11 took
  2.35/2.72 prizes against v8's 1.91 and v9's 2.61; deck-outs are 1 in 89.

## What the rating-controlled model says

Logistic regression, `won ~ opponent_rating/400 + is_v11 + went_first`, over
216 rated games (v11a+v11b against v8+v9):

| term | coef | se | z | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| opponent_rating/400 | -3.1877 | 0.7429 | -4.29 | 0.0000 |
| is_v11 | **-0.7417** | 0.3766 | -1.97 | **0.0489** |
| went_first | +0.7593 | 0.3010 | +2.52 | 0.0116 |

The rating slope converts the v11 coefficient to **-93.1 Elo**. Taken alone
that is a near-exact match for the observed drop. It must not be taken alone:

* the identical-code v11a/v11b control returns -75.5 Elo on the same model;
* v9 against v8 returns -21.6 Elo at p=0.66, i.e. the ranker swap that
  separates them is invisible at this sample size;
* the estimate extrapolates v11 into the 1000+ band it never played.

Direct standardization to a common opponent distribution, which does not
extrapolate, disagrees with the regression and with itself: v11a 0.436
(covering only 72% of the reference), v11b 0.703, v8 0.714, v9 0.605. A single
1/1 game carries 26% of the weight. The two methods bracket zero.

Per-game rating movement confirms both runs were as converged as v8 was:
39-43 points/game over games 1-20, falling to 6.5-6.8 by games 41-45.

## The search layer is not the mechanism

`scripts/probe_grimmsnarl_v11_ladder_overrides.py` walks v8 and v11 side by
side, both teacher-forced, over v11's own 89 ladder games - the states v11
actually reached, which the pre-ship counterfactual on teacher replays could
not use.

* **7,873 decisions, 70 overrides: 0.89% of decisions, 0.79 per game.**
* Games containing an override went **32/49 (65.3%)**; games with none went
  **22/40 (55.0%)**, Fisher p=0.385. The overrides sit on the *winning* side.
* Shipped config verified live: `min_turn=1, top_k=3, determinizations=2`,
  matching `metadata.json`.

A layer that changes 1% of decisions and correlates positively with winning
cannot account for 90 Elo. The correlation direction is confounded - a game
with more turns offers more search opportunities and is also a game we were
not blown out of - but there is no signal pointing the other way.

The dominant override class is a coherent behavioural change worth recording:
**36 of 70 overrides are v8 wanting to play Munkidori and v11 declining**
(28 to a non-card option, 4 to Marnie's Grimmsnarl ex, 4 to Darkness). Games
containing that class went 20/29 (69.0%). Given
`grimmsnarl-counter-engine-tax`, suppressing Munkidori development is a
plausible way to lose games, but this data does not show it losing them.

### A harness caveat that limits the above

On the 70 divergent decisions, the action actually played on the ladder matched
**v8's answer 49 times and v11's only 13**. The deployed agent *was* v11, so
the offline harness does not reproduce live behaviour on exactly the decisions
the new layer controls.

The cause is not the flag leak it first looks like: `arithmetic_search`
line 620 does set `ranker.teacher_forced = False`, but
`save_dynamic_state`/`restore_dynamic_state` carry the flag and the `finally`
block restores it - verified by replaying a full episode and asserting the flag
afterwards. The measured cause is coverage: the harness walks only single-pick
selects, so **3.7% of our decisions - every multi-pick, including Punk Up's
five-energy search - are never fed to the ranker's intra-turn history or the
planner's per-turn budget.** Aggregate reproduction stays high (v8 96.81%,
v11 96.35%) but drifts precisely where the search's candidate margins are thin.

This flaw is inherited from `probe_grimmsnarl_v10_residual.py` and applies to
the pre-ship v11 counterfactual as well, so the "v11 changed 7 of 676
decisions" figure in `RESULTS.md` is not reliable at decision granularity.

## Where v11 actually looks worse

The one matchup with a directional, repeated signal is the one v11 was
commissioned to fix:

| | v8 | v9 | v11 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alakazam, all | 4/11 0.36 | 11/17 0.65 | 9/20 0.45 |
| Alakazam, going first | 4/6 0.67 | 8/10 0.80 | 7/11 0.64 |
| Alakazam, going second | 0/5 0.00 | 3/7 0.43 | **2/9 0.22** |
| mean Alakazam opp rating | 968 | 969 | **866** |

v11 is worse than v9 against Alakazam while facing pilots rated 103 points
lower, and the deficit is in the going-second half. The search layer barely
engages there: 0.3 overrides per game against 0.86 in the mirror, firing in
only 6 of 20 games. So v11 did not fail to fix the matchup by choosing wrong -
**it fails to fix it because the layer almost never fires in it.**

Note the correction this forces on `grimmsnarl-alakazam-is-turn-order`: v8's
0/5 is now 2/9 for v11 and 3/7 for v9. The family is bad going second across
all three versions, but "0-5" was a 5-game artifact.

Turn-order gap overall closed from v8's +0.351 to v11's +0.122, but v9 was
already at +0.147, and v11a/v11b disagree (+0.004 vs +0.232).

## Lineage: v11 is not a descendant of v9

Verified by SHA-256 over the agent directories:

* v9 = v8 with **only** `ranker_model.json` replaced (`b0397a4a`, 1485 trees,
  unconditioned top-4 consensus).
* v11 = v8 with `main.py`, `ml_runtime.py` and the new `arithmetic_search.py`;
  ranker is byte-identical to v8's (`dabc1589`).

## Verdict

The 100-point drop is real as a rating number and not established as a strength
regression. Decomposed:

1. **~45 games against 77**, with the last-20 rating still moving 6-7
   points/game.
2. **An opponent draw with no overlap with the baseline's** - zero games in the
   band where rating is made. Opponent-submission overlap between any two of
   the four runs is 0-2 of ~45.
3. **A ±76 Elo same-code noise floor** at this sample size, which is 80% of the
   effect.
4. A residual, genuinely suggestive **-93 Elo** (p=0.049) that four independent
   methods cannot agree on the sign or size of.

The search layer is exonerated as the cause: 0.89% of decisions, positive
outcome correlation, no timeouts, no illegality.
